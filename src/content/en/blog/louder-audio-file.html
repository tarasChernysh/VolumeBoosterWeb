To make an audio file louder on iPhone, import it into EQfy - Louder Volume from the Files app, open it, and raise the Volume Boost dial from 100% toward 200%, which adds up to +12 dB. Then tap the share button in the top-right corner. EQfy renders a new, louder copy with your settings baked in and opens the iOS share sheet, where you can save it to Files, AirDrop it or send it in a message.

Before you start

  • Audio files only. EQfy works with audio such as MP3 and M4A. It can't import video, so it can't make a quiet video louder.
  • Unprotected files only. Songs from an Apple Music subscription are copy-protected and can't be boosted. Files you downloaded, recorded, bought or synced work.
  • It boosts the copy. EQfy copies the file into the app and exports a new one, so your original isn't touched.

Step by step

  1. Import the file. In EQfy, choose Files and pick the audio file. The steps, including the Music library route, are in our guide to playing MP3 files on iPhone offline.
  2. Open it in the Volume tab. The Volume Boost dial goes from 100% to 200%. Turn it up in small steps while the file plays.
  3. Add bass or EQ if you need them. They're optional and part of Premium. Bass Boost can help a thin recording, and the equalizer can clear up muddy speech.
  4. Tap the share button. It's the arrow-out-of-a-box icon in the top-right corner of the player. A "Saving…" card with a progress ring shows while EQfy renders the file. Keep the app open until it finishes.
  5. Save or send it. When the iOS share sheet opens, choose Save to Files, AirDrop, Messages or any other app.

What ends up in the shared file

The shared file includes the volume boost, the 7-band equalizer, the bass boost and the playback speed you set, plus EQfy's peak limiter. It does not include balance, reverb or 3D Audio, which only apply while you listen in the app. The new file is named after the song with volume_booster added, and it isn't added back to your EQfy library, so save it from the share sheet.

Leave the speed at 1.0× unless you want a copy at a different tempo, and play the shared file once to check that it sounds right.

How much louder can you go?

The dial's top setting adds +12 dB of digital gain. That's a big step, and louder isn't always better: boosting a file that's already near full volume can clip, which sounds crunchy. A few tips:

  • Test at low volume first. A boosted file can be much louder than you expect.
  • Raise in small steps. Stop as soon as the sound turns harsh or fuzzy.
  • Quiet recordings gain the most. A voice note recorded far from the microphone is a good candidate. A mastered song at full volume has little headroom left.
  • Protect your ears. Don't listen to boosted files at high volume for long. Read is a volume booster safe for ears and speakers?

If EQfy isn't the right tool

If your quiet file is a video, EQfy can't help; a video-capable booster is the right tool, and the options are compared in our volume booster guide. If the problem is the volume of apps like Spotify or YouTube rather than a file, no booster can fix that, and our louder-than-max guide covers the iPhone settings that do.

What EQfy can't do: iOS only lets EQfy process audio played inside the app. It can't boost Spotify, YouTube, Apple Music streaming, calls or the system volume, and it isn't a hearing aid. See how Volume Boost works.
