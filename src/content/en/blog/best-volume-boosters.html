The best volume booster app for iPhone depends on what you want to make louder. For songs you play in the app, EQfy - Louder Volume adds up to +12 dB for free. For a quiet video or voice recording, Increase Volume Sound boosts the file itself. For a full music player with a 16-band equalizer, Boom is the bigger option. No booster app can make Spotify, YouTube or calls louder, because iOS doesn't allow it.

We make EQfy, so we've kept this to facts you can check. App details come from each app's US App Store listing, and ratings and features are as of October 21, 2026. Where another app does more than ours, we say so.

One limit every booster app shares

iOS lets an app change only the audio that it plays itself. That means a volume booster works on the songs, video or audio files you open inside it. None of them can make Spotify, YouTube, Netflix, phone calls, notifications or the system volume louder. Songs from an Apple Music subscription are protected, and iOS doesn't let third-party apps process protected audio either; purchased and imported files work.

Check this before you install anything: if an app's listing promises to boost "everything" on your iPhone, test it with a short clip before you pay. Nothing in iOS gives an app that power. The built-in settings that really change other apps' volume are in our guide to making your iPhone louder than max volume.

EQfy - Louder Volume: free boost for your own songs

EQfy is built around the most common problem: songs that are too quiet at full iPhone volume. Open a track from your Music library or the Files app, and the Volume Boost dial adds digital gain from 100% to 200%, which equals up to +12 dB. A peak limiter after the equalizer helps reduce harsh clipping, although a small speaker can still distort if you push it. The dial, the music library, free music discovery and the offline player are free with no time limit.

The extras (Bass Boost, a 7-band equalizer, balance, 3D Audio, export) are part of Premium, and the AI Studio tools use credits. EQfy is a music tool, so it does not boost video. If you mainly want a quick free boost for songs, this is the simplest of the five.

Boom: a full player with a 16-band equalizer

Boom is the most complete player in this list. Its App Store listing describes a bass booster, an 8- or 16-band custom equalizer with 29 presets, Headphone EQ for more than 5,000 headphone models, 3D surround sound, TIDAL, radio stations and podcasts. It is the better choice if you want one app for all your listening. The listing doesn't describe a separate volume-boost control, so it suits people who want richer sound more than raw loudness. We compare it in detail in EQfy vs Boom.

Increase Volume Sound: boost a file, not a player

Increase Volume Sound takes a different approach. According to its listing, you import a video from Photos or an audio file from Files, set the loudness from 1× to 10×, compare the original and boosted versions, and save or share a louder copy. That makes it the right tool for a quiet screen recording, a voice note or a video you want to send. EQfy can't do that for video. The listing says it works on imported media and doesn't change calls, system volume or other apps, and that it offers a subscription with plan limits on exports.

A 10× boost is a lot. Preview the result at a low volume first, because very loud files can clip and hurt your ears.

Equalizer Fx: a 7-band EQ and bass booster

Equalizer Fx from TwinBit is the most-rated app here, with about 28,000 ratings. Its listing centres on a 7-band equalizer, bass boosting, more than 15 presets and custom presets, with monthly and yearly subscriptions. It says plainly that effects can't be applied to protected Apple Music tracks, which is the same iOS limit EQfy has. Choose it if you mostly want a bass booster and a preset-based EQ rather than a loudness dial.

Higher Volume: a simple booster, with few details

Higher Volume: Louder Boost has a simple pitch. Its listing promises to push your phone's volume higher for "music to videos, podcasts, and more", with custom audio settings and a subscription. The listing doesn't explain which audio it covers or how, and we haven't tested it, so run a short test before paying. Remember that iOS doesn't let any app change other apps' audio.

Which one should you pick?

  • Songs sound quiet: EQfy, for a free boost of up to +12 dB.
  • A video or voice recording is quiet: Increase Volume Sound, which boosts the file and gives you a louder copy.
  • You want a full player with deep EQ and headphone profiles: Boom.
  • You want a preset-based EQ and bass booster: Equalizer Fx. Read our EQ settings guide first, so you know what to set.

Before you install anything, check the free fixes: lift the speaker Volume Limit, relax Reduce Loud Audio for headphones, turn off Sound Check in the Music app, set the EQ to Off, and clean the speaker grille. They take a few minutes and solve many "my iPhone is too quiet" cases. The full list is in iPhone Volume Too Low? 12 Quick Fixes to Try.
