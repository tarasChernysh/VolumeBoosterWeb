To make a voice memo louder on iPhone, save it from Voice Memos to the Files app, import it into EQfy - Louder Volume, raise the Volume Boost dial from 100% toward 200% (up to +12 dB), then tap the share button to save a louder copy. Voice Memos itself has no volume boost, only playback options such as Enhance Recording, which reduces background noise and echo while you listen. Your original recording stays untouched.

Why is a voice memo so quiet?

A quiet voice memo is usually quiet in the file. The phone may have been far from the speaker, the voice soft, or the room noisy, so the microphone captured a low level. Raising your iPhone's volume makes it easier to hear on your phone, but it doesn't change the recording, so the memo is just as quiet when you share it or play it somewhere else. What Voice Memos can do

Apple's Voice Memos guide lists these playback options, found under a recording's Options:

  • Enhance Recording reduces background noise and echo when you play Mono and Stereo recordings.
  • Skip Silence skips the gaps in Mono and Stereo recordings.
  • Playback speed plays the recording faster or slower.

Apple says these settings don't affect the recording itself. Its guide doesn't say whether an exported file includes Enhance Recording, so listen to your exported copy before you rely on it. Turning Enhance Recording on is still worth trying first, because less noise makes a boosted voice easier to understand. None of these options makes a recording louder, and the guide lists no volume or gain control.

How to make a voice memo louder with EQfy

  1. Export the memo to Files. In Voice Memos, tap the recording, tap the More (…) button, tap Share, scroll down and tap Save to Files, then choose a folder and tap Save. Apple's steps are in Export a Voice Memos recording to Files. Keep the default .m4a format. The Editable option uses a QuickTime Audio file instead, which we haven't tested with EQfy.
  2. Import it into EQfy. In EQfy, choose Files and pick the recording. The EQfy picker accepts audio files one at a time. More on importing is in playing MP3 files on iPhone offline.
  3. Open it and raise Volume Boost. In the Volume tab, turn the dial from 100% toward 200% in small steps while the memo plays. Stop when the voice is clear but not harsh.
  4. Share the louder copy. Tap the share button in the top-right corner of the player. A "Saving…" card shows while EQfy renders the file. Then save it to Files, AirDrop it or send it in a message.

The new file is named after the recording with volume_booster added, and the volume boost is baked in. The full list of what ends up in a shared file is in how to make an audio file louder on iPhone.

Tips for a clearer, louder voice

  • Boost in steps. Voice recordings can distort when pushed too far. EQfy's peak limiter helps reduce harsh clipping, but it can't fix a recording that is already clipped.
  • Expect noise to rise too. Volume Boost lifts everything, so hiss and room noise get louder along with the voice. For a very noisy recording, read how to remove background noise from audio on iPhone.
  • Add some EQ if the voice is muddy. The equalizer is part of Premium, and the settings in our EQ guide are a starting point. Lifting the upper-mid bands often helps speech cut through.
  • Check your volume. A boosted memo can be much louder than you expect, especially in headphones. Start low, and see is volume boosting safe?

What it can't do

  • It can't fix a recording that is too noisy or distorted. Boosting makes the loud parts louder too.
  • It can't change the memo inside Voice Memos. You get a new, louder copy in Files or wherever you share it; the original stays as it was.
  • It works on audio, not video. EQfy doesn't import video, so it can't boost a quiet screen recording.
  • The Noise Reducer tool takes MP3 or WAV only. Voice Memos recordings are .m4a, so they'd need converting first. See the noise guide above.
What EQfy can't do: iOS only lets EQfy process audio played inside the app, so it can't boost Spotify, YouTube, Apple Music streaming, calls or the system volume. It isn't a hearing aid. See how Volume Boost works.
