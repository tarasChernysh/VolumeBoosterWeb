iPhone Volume Keeps Going Down? Why and How to Fix

By the EQfy team · Published October 24, 2026 · 4 min read

When your iPhone volume keeps going down by itself, one of its automatic features is usually responsible: a headphone notification that sets a lower volume the next time you connect, Reduce Loud Audio, AirPods features like Conversation Awareness, Attention Aware Features (which lower alert volume when you look at the phone), or Sound Check in the Music app. If the volume drops while you're not touching anything, also check whether a case or your pocket is pressing the volume button.

1. A headphone notification lowered the volume

Apple's guide to checking your headphone audio levels says that if you listen to loud headphone audio long enough to affect your hearing, iPhone sends a notification that you should turn down the volume. After a notification, the next time you plug in your headphones or connect them over Bluetooth, your volume is automatically set to a lower level. You can turn it up again.

To see what happened, open Health, tap Browse › Hearing › Headphone Notifications. Apple notes that depending on your country or region, Headphone Notifications may be on by default and may not be possible to turn off. Where it's allowed, the switch is in Settings › Sounds & Haptics › Headphone Safety.

2. Reduce Loud Audio is set too low

In the same Headphone Safety screen, Reduce Loud Audio makes iPhone analyze your headphone audio and reduce any sound above the level you set. If the slider is low, loud parts of songs get turned down, and it can feel as if the volume keeps dropping. Raise the slider a step or two instead of switching it off, because it's there to protect your hearing. Our guide iPhone volume too low? 12 quick fixes also covers the speaker's Volume Limit and Reduce Loud Sounds.

3. AirPods features that change media volume

On supported AirPods, two features adjust your media for you. Conversation Awareness lowers your media when you start speaking and raises it again after the conversation ends. Personalized Volume adjusts media volume based on your surroundings and listening habits. To turn either off, wear your AirPods, open Settings and tap their name, then switch the option off (check Audio & Routing if you don't see it). Apple explains the controls in Adjust listening mode settings, and our guide to making AirPods louder has more.

4. Attention Aware Features lower alert volume

If your ringtone or alerts get quieter when you pick up the phone and look at it, that's by design. Apple's article on Attention Aware features says that when attention is detected, your iPhone lowers the volume of your alerts. These features use the TrueDepth camera, even if you haven't set up Face ID. They change alerts, not the music or video you're playing.

To turn them off, Apple says to open Settings, tap Face ID & Passcode and turn off Attention Aware Features. Menu names can move between iOS versions, so if you don't see it, search Settings for "Attention".

5. Sound Check and app volume normalization

Some apps even out loudness between songs, which can make a loud song get turned down. Apple's Sound Check article says it adjusts the loudness between different songs to play at the same volume; on iPhone it's under Settings › Apps › Music. Spotify has a similar volume setting that is described in our Spotify guide. If volume changes only in one app, look at that app's settings first.

6. A pressed volume button

If the volume drops when the phone is in a pocket, a bag or a snug case, the volume-down button may be getting pressed. Take the case off, press each volume button once to check it moves freely, and watch the on-screen volume indicator when the drop happens. If the button feels stuck or the volume changes with nothing touching it, a repair shop or Apple Support can check it. Also check the device you're connected to, because a Bluetooth speaker or car stereo may change its own volume (Bluetooth volume low?).

What about boosting the volume back up?

Raising the iPhone volume again is the simplest fix. If a particular song or recording is quiet in the file, EQfy - Louder Volume can boost it by up to +12 dB inside the app, and our guide to making an iPhone louder than max volume explains how. EQfy doesn't control the iOS features above: it can't stop a headphone notification, Reduce Loud Audio or Attention Aware from changing volume. Frequently asked questions

Why does my iPhone volume keep going down by itself?

iPhone has several features that lower volume automatically: a headphone notification that sets a lower volume the next time you connect, Reduce Loud Audio, AirPods features such as Conversation Awareness and Personalized Volume, and Attention Aware Features, which lower the volume of alerts. Sound Check can also make some songs quieter. A pressed volume button is another cause.

Why does my iPhone volume go down when I pick it up?

Apple says that when attention is detected, iPhone lowers the volume of your alerts. This is Attention Aware Features, which use the TrueDepth camera on Face ID iPhones. It affects alerts such as ringtones, not your music. You can turn it off in Face ID & Passcode settings.

Why is my volume lower every time I connect headphones?

Apple says that after iPhone sends you a notification about loud headphone listening, the next time you plug in or connect headphones over Bluetooth your volume is set to a lower level automatically. You can turn the volume up again. Check Headphone Notifications and Reduce Loud Audio in Settings › Sounds & Haptics › Headphone Safety.

Should I turn these features off?

Only if they get in your way. Headphone notifications and Reduce Loud Audio exist to protect your hearing, so raise the limit a step before you switch them off, and keep the level moderate. Is volume boosting safe? explains the risks.
