How to Make iPhone Louder Than Max Volume

By the EQfy team · Published October 11, 2026 · 6 min read

To make iPhone louder than max, first remove the settings that quietly cap it (Volume Limit, Reduce Loud Audio and Sound Check), then make sure the Music app's EQ isn't cutting the sound and that the speaker is clean and uncovered. For songs you own, a booster app such as EQfy - Louder Volume adds up to +12 dB on top of the iPhone's own maximum, and that part is free.

Why your iPhone isn't as loud as it could be

Your iPhone's maximum volume is set by its hardware, and no setting turns a phone speaker into a party speaker. What you can change is everything that sits between the song and that ceiling. Several iOS features lower the maximum without telling you. Some songs are mastered much quieter than others. And a case, dust or your own hand can muffle the speaker more than you'd think.

So the fastest way to get more volume is to work in this order: remove the limits, use the built-in loudness tricks, fix the physical stuff, and only then add extra gain with an app.

7 built-in ways to make your iPhone louder

All the menu paths below match current iOS versions. Apple moved many app settings under Settings › Apps, so if you're on an older version the Music options may sit directly under Settings.

1. Turn off the speaker Volume Limit

Since iOS 18.2 you can cap the built-in speaker. The cap may have been switched on at some point, or set through a family Screen Time setup.

  1. Open Settings › Sounds & Haptics.
  2. Tap Volume Limit (under Built-In Speaker on newer versions).
  3. Turn off Limit Maximum Volume, or drag the slider all the way up.

On the latest iOS you may also see Reduce Loud Sounds under Built-In Speaker. Apple describes it as softening loud sounds from the speaker. If music sounds flat and lacks punch, try it off. Apple's Adjust the volume on iPhone page shows both settings.

2. Check Reduce Loud Audio for headphones

Headphone Safety caps headphone volume at a decibel level you choose. Go to Settings › Sounds & Haptics › Headphone Safety and look at Reduce Loud Audio. If it's on with a low limit, raise the slider a step. This is a hearing-protection feature, so don't switch it off without thinking.

Two related things can also lower the volume:

  • Headphone notifications. If iPhone warns you that you've been listening loud for long enough to affect your hearing, it sets the volume lower the next time you connect headphones. You can turn it back up yourself.
  • Bluetooth speakers counted as headphones. A speaker or car stereo that iPhone treats as headphones may fall under the same cap. Set the right type in Settings › Bluetooth › ⓘ › Device Type.

Apple explains these settings in Check your headphone audio levels on iPhone.

3. Check the EQ in the Music app

Go to Settings › Apps › Music › EQ. Presets that cut frequencies, such as Bass Reducer or Treble Reducer, make songs quieter, so choose Off unless a preset really suits your speaker or headphones. Older guides recommend the Late Night preset for extra loudness, but it's no longer in the EQ list on current iOS versions. Apple lists EQ as a Music app setting (Apple's EQ and Sound Check guide), so it only affects songs you play in the Music app.

4. Turn off Sound Check

Sound Check adjusts songs to play at the same volume, which can mean turning louder tracks down. Apple's support article If music in Apple Music sounds quiet points to this setting first. Turn it off in Settings › Apps › Music › Sound Check. Other apps have the same idea. Spotify, for example, has an audio normalization setting with a Loud level for Premium users.

5. Clean the speaker grilles

Lint and dust packed into the bottom grille absorb sound. Apple's guide for no sound or distorted sound from the iPhone speaker says to check the speaker opening (and the receiver at the top) for blockage, and clean it with a small, soft-bristled brush that's clean and dry. Avoid pins and liquids.

6. Remove the case and aim the speaker at you

Apple also suggests removing cases, films and screen protectors when sound seems blocked. A thick case can cover part of the grille. Then check how you hold the phone. In landscape it's easy to cup the bottom speaker with your palm. Pointing the speaker toward you, or standing the phone near a hard surface so sound reflects your way, can help, and it costs nothing.

7. Turn up your Bluetooth speaker or car stereo

Many Bluetooth speakers, headphones and car stereos have their own volume control. If the accessory is set low, maxing out the iPhone won't help. Turn up both, and check the Device Type setting from step 2.

Make it sound louder with Bass and EQ

Loudness isn't only about level. Your ears are most sensitive in the midrange, and small speakers lose the lowest bass. So shaping the sound can make music feel louder without pushing everything up:

  • Bass Boost adds up to +12 dB around 200 Hz. That's the upper bass a small iPhone speaker can actually reproduce, so you hear more punch. See how to boost bass on iPhone.
  • The 7-band equalizer (32 Hz to 16 kHz plus a preamp) lets you lift the 1 kHz and 4 kHz bands so vocals and guitars cut through. Our best EQ settings guide lists the exact values for all 9 presets.

Keep it loud, but safe

More volume is great on a noisy train or at a picnic. It's not great for your ears if it's in headphones for hours. The World Health Organization says adults can listen at 80 dB for up to 40 hours a week, and the safe time drops quickly as the level rises (WHO safe listening Q&A). Boost gradually, take breaks, and use the extra headroom for quiet recordings rather than for blasting everything. Our guide Is a volume booster safe? goes into detail.

Still too quiet?

If your iPhone is quiet even with every limit off, something else is going on. It could be a Bluetooth device still connected, a Balance slider moved to one side, or a speaker that needs service. Frequently asked questions

Can you make an iPhone louder than its max volume?

Not for the whole system. iOS doesn't let any app raise the volume of other apps, calls or system sounds. You can remove settings that keep your iPhone below its real maximum, and a booster app like EQfy can add up to +12 dB to songs you play inside it.

Does EQfy make Spotify or YouTube louder?

No. iOS only lets EQfy process audio played inside EQfy, so it can't boost Spotify, YouTube, Apple Music streaming, phone or FaceTime calls, notifications or system volume. Import songs from your Music library or the Files app to boost them.

Is EQfy's Volume Boost free?

Yes. EQfy is free to download and the Volume Boost dial (100% to 200%, up to +12 dB) is free. Bass Boost, the 7-band equalizer and the other player tools are part of the optional Premium subscription.

Why can't I boost my Apple Music songs in EQfy?

Songs from an Apple Music subscription are DRM-protected. They play in EQfy, but iOS doesn't allow apps to process protected audio, so the enhancement tools aren't available for them. Purchased, unprotected songs and files you import work with every tool.

Will boosting the volume damage my iPhone speaker?

Boosting adds digital gain, and the warning sign that you've gone too far is distortion: crackling, buzzing or harsh sound. Turn the boost down as soon as you hear it. Our volume safety guide covers both your speaker and your ears.
